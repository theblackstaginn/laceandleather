Settlement Planning, Landmarks, and Map Hierarchy

Settlements work best when their placement tells a geographic story. Rivers, coasts, crossroads, defensive ground, farmland, and trade routes can all explain why a town exists where it does. This field note uses a Mapmaker Sheets surface to explore settlement placement and visual hierarchy, with enough breathing room for landmarks, names, roads, and later campaign notes.

Before placing a settlement, ask what keeps it alive. Water, food, movement, defense, trade, religion, industry, and politics all leave fingerprints on location.

Once the location makes sense, hierarchy keeps the page readable. Capitals, towns, villages, ruins, and landmarks do not all need equal visual weight.

An aged planning sheet gives you room to test that hierarchy before committing to final artwork. It also leaves space for the practical notes that explain what the symbols mean.
